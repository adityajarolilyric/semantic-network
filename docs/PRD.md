# Product requirements

## Transaction-led population workflow

- Author valid populations inside Transactions, either from its distinct input rows, from selected population tables/Cartesian factors/projections, or by reusing a saved population. **Reason:** remove a separate compound-Dimension setup step while preserving valid intersections.
- Keep member catalogs under Dimensions and reusable population definitions in existing `dimension` metadata. **Reason:** simplify authoring without changing identities or execution contracts.
- Suggest matching key names, allow independent populations, and never infer equal coverage from equal grain. **Reason:** Forecast and Sales can share keys but cover different combinations.
- Select Base on the population or centrally on the Transactions list; persist the selection once per revision. **Reason:** Base is a shared valid population, not a per-table flag or an implied join path.
- Save population, Transaction, selected/rebound Measures and Base atomically with optimistic versions. Preview unsaved drafts without metadata writes. **Reason:** incomplete saves must not leave orphan populations or inconsistent measure context.
- Retain configured aggregation, optional relationships, multi-grain/date/pivot/filter semantics, incremental builds, edit policies and current-table storage. **Reason:** reducing setup effort must preserve business results.

Status: product baseline for review, 4 November 2026. The current contract is derived from the supplied Semantic demo, the accepted design decisions and the repository implementation. Future items and unresolved decisions are labelled separately; this document does not claim that the prototype is already a fully operated production service.

The product lets a modeller turn heterogeneous supply-chain tables into an explicit semantic network without writing custom entity SQL for the supported transformations. Planners then analyse compatible measures at different grains and edit them where business authority permits. Definitions must preserve enough identity, lineage, policy and explanation for another person or a future agent to understand the model without guessing business rules.

The companion [User flow](USER_FLOW.md) provides the end-to-end demand-planning walkthrough. Detailed engineering references remain in [the design guide](README.md).

## 1. Problem and desired outcome

Forecasts, sales and promotions can describe the same business at different grains and formats. One source uses Product/Location records, another Brand/Zone, another mixed detail and aggregate assignments. Attributes can arrive as separate rows, and dates can arrive as ranges. Joining these facts directly can multiply quantities, omit valid planning intersections, misapply percentages or permit edits below the source's authority.

The desired outcome is one declared interpretation of dimensions, valid intersections, transaction resolution, measures and policies. Supported transformations are configured as metadata, executed consistently by the backend, and explainable in previews and planning results. Unsupported or ambiguous interpretations fail visibly instead of selecting arbitrary values or silently dropping business data.

### Users and responsibilities

- **Semantic modeller:** registers inputs, defines axes/intersections, chooses policies and validates the model.
- **Planner or analyst:** selects a view, interprets measures and edits eligible referenced values.
- **Operator:** runs the application, controls resources, handles recovery and intentional workspace reset.
- **Future agent consumer:** reads stable semantic definitions, examples, evidence and capabilities. Autonomous agent interaction is not an implemented user journey.

These are conceptual responsibilities. User accounts, role-based permissions and approval workflows are not implied by these personas.

### Core terms

- **Source:** the original physical data table. Registering or transforming its semantic use does not rewrite its rows.
- **Managed input:** the revision-specific saved view through which semantic objects consume a Source. It remains an architectural concept even though its separate navigation item is hidden.
- **Dimension:** a business axis such as Product, Location or Time, with member keys and descriptive attributes. Brand is an attribute of Product, not automatically a separate Dimension.
- **Dimension:** a declared grain plus its valid population and selected descriptive attributes. Grain is the complete key tuple; population is the set of permitted tuples.
- **Base Dimension:** the revision's selected finest connected planning representation. It does not make independent planning domains invalid.
- **Native or Output Dimension:** the level at which a measure is defined and its edit authority originates. Referenced measures inherit this from their Transaction.
- **Representation:** the measure values available at a particular dimension after building. Availability at a finer grain does not grant edit authority there.
- **Aggregation versus distribution:** aggregation combines existing values; distribution maps an assignment or edit into finer cells. The three distribution contracts in section 4 have separate purposes and policies.

Three business axes can require more than three key columns. In the demo, Product, Location and Time use Product ID, Location ID, Week ID and Month because the supplied Time identity is composite.

### Product goals

1. Complete the demonstrated Forecast/Sales/Promotion workflow through metadata and UI.
2. Preserve meaning across grains, NULL values, date expansion, calculations and edits.
3. Reuse materialised representations when correct and explain fallbacks when they are needed.
4. Make preview, metadata Save, population Build, network Build and interactive Edit distinguishable.
5. Keep published definitions, active results and failed candidates isolated.
6. Keep modelling intent independent of display labels, physical connection settings and scenarios.

### Current non-goals

General transformation scripting, arbitrary SQL execution, rigid hierarchy authoring, automatic schema reconciliation, source/master-data row editing, persistent resolved-transaction caches, scenarios, durable edit replay, shared server-saved pivot layouts, Decision View query-time filters, transitive Drill To and development-to-production promotion are outside the current contract. Equality-based Drill To is a [proposal](RELATIONSHIPS.md), not a delivered capability. The local Compose deployment does not provide production HA, end-user identity or an enterprise access boundary.

## 2. Interpret the demo precisely

The following clarifications prevent demo shorthand from becoming an incorrect requirement:

- **Base belongs to a revision.** There is at most one selected Base in each revision; another revision can choose differently. It is not a permanent model-wide level flag.
- **Base connectivity is conditional.** Coarser measures move to Base only where a path is proven. Independent native levels and their compatible coarser views remain valid.
- **Calendar identity is business-specific.** The demo needs Week ID plus Month. Another calendar may require Year or a globally unique period ID. DISTINCT cannot repair conflicting non-key attributes.
- **Precomputation reduces joins; it does not promise join-free reads.** Proven runtime enrichment, formula planning and correctness fallbacks can still be needed.
- **Three distribution decisions exist.** Transaction Split/Copy, source-build finer visibility and interactive edit allocation have different purposes. They must never be substituted for one another.
- **Storage is a pair per level.** Two fully built dimensions can use four planning/value physical tables, not two tables for the whole model. No additional planning/value staging table is allowed during rebuilds.
- **Calculated dependency chains are supported.** The final-forecast example references a calculated discount quantity. Valid chains are current scope; cyclic or unavailable dependencies fail. Each node retains its own AggregateFirst/CalculateFirst boundary. CalculateFirst may consume AggregateFirst at its own output grain; all transitive inputs must be available there. The picker explains incompatible/cyclic inputs, and source edits or formula changes refresh downstream consumers. **Reason:** inlining nested formulas or checking only immediate neighbours can change results or admit an invalid grain path.
- **UI labels are not semantics.** Managed Tables is hidden from current navigation; its availability/view mechanism remains. Business rules survive later UI redesign.
- **Counts are fixture observations.** Eight active products, 576 planning intersections and 154 promotion combinations are demo-specific, not acceptance constants for arbitrary inputs.
- **A promotion percentage is a modeller's business interpretation.** Copy, AVG and Repeat are suitable for the demonstrated constant-percentage use case only when explicitly chosen. The discount/reduction formulas do not encode a general causal model of promotions.

## 3. Product rules and rationale

Every rule below states the required behaviour and its reason. Stable identifiers make the rules traceable from the user flow and acceptance cases.

### Context, lifecycle and authoring

- **P01 — Single tenant, single working context.** Expose one default Semantic Model and Revision; retain scoped MongoDB metadata for future multi-model/revision support. Store no tenant identifier, physical connection binding or database credential in semantic definitions. **Reason:** business meaning is portable and does not duplicate server configuration.
- **P02 — Default working context.** An empty installation creates a default model and first draft through idempotent bootstrap, with no published revision or Base. Current public APIs cannot create additional models/revisions or delete the defaults. Existing historical metadata is preserved. **Reason:** the first user can begin immediately while the active editing context stays unambiguous.
- **P03 — Automatic context, no publication.** Remove model/revision selectors, creation and publication from the UI and remove their creation, deletion and publish endpoints from production APIs. Keep GET/list, metadata update and validation APIs; validate from Sources. Retain lifecycle schemas/services internally for future support. **Reason:** the current flow needs one editable context, and publication would freeze its only revision. Database scope avoids a future metadata migration when lifecycle support returns.
- **P04 — Revision-owned Base.** Persist only the revision's selected Base reference, not independent isBase flags on levels. Drafts may omit it temporarily; current publication and network building require a valid selection. **Reason:** multiple level flags could disagree, while drafts need an incremental authoring path.
- **P05 — Stable identities and labels.** Use owner-qualified stable object/column IDs for references and lineage. Hide technical IDs/physical names in routine UI. Source names remain physical names; Dimension columns can have separate display names. Dimension root name is enough; no extra root display-name control. **Reason:** renames must not silently change relationships or confuse users with execution internals.
- **P06 — No inferred schema migration.** Stable IDs support a future controlled rename/reconciliation process, but current registration does not automatically detect/rebind external renames, reorderings or schema drift. Never recycle a dropped identity. **Reason:** similar labels cannot prove that a new physical column is the same business concept.
- **P07 — Metadata is authoritative on the server.** Persist definitions in MongoDB. Browser state holds fetched data, unsaved inputs and experience preferences only; do not persist semantic truth or source rows in browser storage. Save failures/conflicts preserve the form; do not silently replace newer server intent. **Reason:** reloads, agents and concurrent users need one authoritative definition.
- **P08 — Resource-specific actions.** Dimension/Transaction actions are Cancel, Raw Data Preview, Preview and Save metadata; Measure Save captures its definition. Dimension uses Cancel, Preview and Save, with build/commit semantics in B01. Help explains non-obvious policies without backend calls. **Reason:** a user must know which actions merely inspect, define or change active data.

### Source import and managed inputs

- **S01 — Bounded sequential import.** Accept up to ten CSV files per UI batch, process one file per request sequentially, and create a new table for each. Per-file bounds are 5,000 data records, 200 columns, 65,536 characters per cell and the configured size limit, default 10 MiB. **Reason:** the evaluation workflow remains predictable without an unbounded ingestion pipeline.
- **S02 — Preserve input meaning.** Support UTF-8/BOM, standard comma quoting, multiline records and CRLF. Reject malformed encoding/quoting, inconsistent widths, null bytes and duplicate/empty/invalid headers; headers have no outer whitespace/control characters and are at most 128 characters. Do not silently rename or trim fields. **Reason:** silent cleanup can alter keys or conceal a data-quality issue.
- **S03 — Explicit inference limits.** Infer compatible signed 64-bit integers, decimals fitting 38 digits, valid ISO dates within 1900–2299 and lowercase true/false booleans; preserve leading-zero codes, unsupported ranges and mixed values as text. Empty CSV cells, including quoted empty strings, become NULL. Do not infer locale, timezone, units, business keys or alternative delimiters. **Reason:** a plausible conversion is not evidence of business meaning.
- **S04 — No overwrite and safe retry.** Imports do not append to or overwrite an existing named table. Identical table/key/bytes can replay; collisions or changed requests fail. Retry unsuccessful entries without reimporting successful files, and distinguish an import success from a later display-refresh failure. **Reason:** retries must not duplicate or destroy source data. Pending upload queues are browser-owned and do not survive reload.
- **S05 — Uploaded tables are managed by default.** CSV import registers scope and builds the saved revision-specific identity view; existing warehouse tables can still use the Sources grid checkbox. Downstream readers use that view even without transformations; no raw-table fallback. Disabling is dependency-aware and preserves raw data/configuration. **Reason:** consumers need one consistent input contract and safe dependency handling.
- **S06 — Previews are samples with totals.** All table previews show at most 50 records and the full relevant count, including filtered totals. Source paging may inspect another bounded page. Checks cover full inputs; ordering is deterministic for preview, not an assertion of original file order. **Reason:** a small sample must neither overload the UI nor be mistaken for proof of uniqueness or completeness.

- **S07 — Automatic editable metadata defaults.** Uploaded tables without numeric columns become Dimensions keyed by all columns. Tables with numeric columns become standalone Transactions keyed by all non-numeric columns, with one non-editable Sum measure per numeric column. Use the managed table name. Numeric IDs follow this same rule; never guess business roles from names. Numeric-only/over-limit inputs remain scoped and request manual grain setup. Create a Transaction and its measures atomically; replay must preserve user edits. **Reason:** remove repetitive setup while keeping business grain and later Dimension binding under user control. See [upload setup and recovery](SOURCES_AND_MANAGED_TABLES.md#automatic-semantic-setup-after-upload).

### Dimensions and Time

- **D01 — Member identity is declared.** Each Dimension requires at least one nonnullable key column, potentially composite. A declaration is not a certificate that live data was independently validated at metadata Save. **Reason:** member identity must be expressible, but metadata CRUD cannot truthfully claim unperformed warehouse checks.
- **D02 — Exposure is independent.** Start with all Dimension attributes exposed; preserve deselections on reopen. Keys are always exposed. Member label is separate from a column heading/display name. **Reason:** users save clicks without losing control of which business attributes consumers see.
- **D03 — Independent transformations.** A Dimension's Pivot/Filters belong to that object, not to its shared managed input. Filter after Pivot and semantic aliasing, before exposure/consumption. Unexposed attributes remain usable in filters. **Reason:** one consumer's interpretation must not mutate another's data contract.
- **D04 — Dimension filtering does not redefine intersections.** A filtered-out member does not silently remove source population rows. Missing referenced members and conflicting required enrichment fail under lookup contracts. **Reason:** pruning by an accidental join would hide invalid planning combinations.
- **D05 — One explicit Time Dimension.** At most one Dimension per revision is marked isTimeDim. Capture actual calendar base grain, Date column and Ordering column. Base-grain choices are Day/Week/Month; requested views may use supplied coarser calendar attributes. **Reason:** date meaning and temporal ordering cannot be recovered reliably from labels.
- **D06 — Calendar, not date-name inference.** Period attributes and complete period identity come from calendar data, including fiscal calendars. Daily range resolution requires a daily calendar. Do not generate missing calendar days or assume ISO-week/month membership. **Reason:** business calendars can differ from civil-calendar assumptions.

### Pivot transformation

- **T01 — Normalise supported long inputs.** Support multiple attribute/value pairs and discovered output literals. Consumed pair fields do not remain as raw output/key choices; retained fields and generated typed outputs do. Do not silently add newly discovered literals to saved definitions. **Reason:** the downstream schema must be stable and wide enough for explicit mapping.
- **T02 — Same-record grouping.** With no separate-record keys, group retained fields plus pair discriminators/values and produce outputs without spreading one source record into several records. Exact grouping tuples collapse. Retain an occurrence ID to preserve otherwise identical facts. **Reason:** Forecast-style pairs already belong together, but identical visible rows do not encode occurrence identity.
- **T03 — Separate-record merging.** Supply retained record keys when EAV records jointly describe one entity/fact. Other retained fields must be determined by those keys; each selected pivot cell must have one contributing row. Duplicate cells fail even for equal values; never invent SUM/MAX/FIRST. **Reason:** the engine cannot know which duplicate or conflicting attribute is authoritative.
- **T04 — Keys have separate roles.** Pivot record keys identify rows to combine; a Dimension key identifies members; an optional managed-output key only validates duplicate output keys. That optional declaration does not change grouping and does not separately reject NULL keys. **Reason:** conflating these roles caused misleading duplicate-cell failures in the Forecast examples.
- **T05 — Stable output and compatible reads.** Generated output IDs depend on owner/stage/pair/literal, not display alias. Preview and execution use the same operators; old frozen pivot definitions remain readable without reintroducing automatic layout detection. **Reason:** reuse, cloning and label changes must preserve dependencies without rewriting historical semantics.

### Planning populations and grain paths

- **L01 — Mandatory grain.** A Dimension requires selected grain columns. Build one row per non-NULL grain tuple; every Transaction at the level must map all keys. Grain names are inherited rather than user-renamed. **Reason:** grain is the join identity and cannot be implicit or label-dependent.
- **L02 — Preserve valid intersections.** A single intersection input preserves its selected combinations. Cartesian population crosses configured factor relations; equal names do not imply joins. Prefer valid Product/Location pairs × Time over independent Product × Location × Time when sourcing restricts pairs. **Reason:** the latter can manufacture invalid combinations and unnecessary growth.
- **L03 — Distinct parent projection.** A child can retain parent keys and promote selected parent output attributes to grain. Ignore unselected columns and apply final DISTINCT for every population strategy after enrichment/filters. **Reason:** weekly or Brand/Zone intersections should not duplicate merely because the parent also carries daily/detail fields.
- **L04 — Grain determines attributes.** Non-key population/enriched attributes do not change grain and must have one distinct combination per grain tuple. A field cannot simultaneously be key and ordinary population attribute. **Reason:** DISTINCT removes identical rows; it cannot choose between different Months or Categories for one declared key.
- **L05 — Optional, typed Dimension bindings.** A level can have no bindings. A binding can map compatible selected population columns to any relevant attribute of that Dimension, not only its primary key; composite mappings stay intact. **Reason:** standalone populations are useful, while attribute-grain levels need mappings such as Brand → Product.Brand.
- **L06 — Membership differs from enrichment.** With no selected Dimension outputs, validate membership only. With outputs, the mapped tuple must determine their distinct combination. Identical reduced combinations are valid; missing members or conflicting requested combinations fail. Enrichment never silently expands/prunes the population or changes grain. **Reason:** one Brand may match many Products while still determining a single Category.
- **L07 — Explicit lineage proves movement.** The shared analyzer decides equivalent, roll-up, roll-down or incompatible using projection/dimension/time lineage. Key counts, matching names and an FK alone do not prove a measure path. A primary-key binding can resolve further dimension attributes; a non-key binding promises only mapped/selected coordinates. **Reason:** structural similarity is not a functional dependency.
- **L08 — Base is finest among connected levels.** Reject Base if a proven strictly finer related level exists; permit equivalent and independent levels. No universal rule requires every level/measure to connect to Base. **Reason:** distinct planning domains can coexist without inventing missing dimensions or intersections.
- **L09 — Mapping inheritance is conservative.** Inherit a projection's binding only when its complete mapped tuple survives, preserving valid selections and deliberate empty selections. Names remain plain where unique, with deterministic collision aliases and stable lineage. **Reason:** partially retained composite bindings and accumulated prefixes misrepresent identity or obscure business names.

### Transaction mapping and mixed grains

- **X01 — One declared native level.** A Transaction has one native dimension; it consumes the effective schema after its preceding transforms. Uniform coarser data can use its own coarser level; mixed assignments may resolve into a chosen finer native level. **Reason:** output authority and later edit eligibility must be declared, not inferred from individual input rows.
- **X02 — Complete direct keys, repeated details allowed.** Direct mapping covers every native key exactly once. Multiple detail rows are valid; unmatched transaction keys fail rather than being silently dropped. Do not ask users for Expected rows per grain in normal authoring. **Reason:** aggregation handles detail multiplicity, while a left join alone would conceal out-of-population facts.
- **X03 — Aggregate before joining.** Independently aggregate each measure's Transaction by all mapped native keys using its configured policy, then LEFT JOIN to the native population. Never join multiple raw fact streams before aggregation. **Reason:** independent detail multiplicities would multiply unrelated measures, and missing facts must not remove valid planning rows.
- **X04 — Explicit column matching.** New mixed-grain rules use Column equals attribute; pivot Level/Value inputs first. All present restrictions apply together. Every native axis needs a restriction unless Allow all explicitly permits absence; an absent value is not an implicit broadcast. **Reason:** broad assignments must be intentional and conjunctive constraints must not be weakened.
- **X05 — Match real native intersections.** Resolve against actual native rows through proven paths, not independent member-domain Cartesian products. Preserve typed codes and explicit blank-as-absent settings. **Reason:** combinations and identifier semantics belong to the model, not convenient string conversion.
- **X06 — Explicit application order wins.** Higher configured sortable values apply later at any grain; a coarse row can overwrite a detailed row. NULL order and overlapping equal-order ties fail. Text order is lexical, so P10 need not follow P2 numerically. **Reason:** source row order and specificity are not the declared business priority.
- **X07 — Transaction allocation is per value.** Choose Copy or Split for explicitly selected numeric values, independently across members and dates where applicable. Split uses each assignment's original full match counts; later overrides do not redistribute overwritten portions. **Reason:** assignment meaning is independent of measure policy, whether measures are created with the Transaction or later.
- **X08 — Whole winning payload and retained context.** The winner carries its payload, including NULL; no fallback to an earlier non-NULL assignment. Retained columns are available for preview, ordering and final filters, but do not automatically become population attributes or Decision View dimensions. A distributed value cannot also control order. **Reason:** precedence and context must not create hidden measure fill rules or circular allocation decisions.
- **X09 — Distinguish replacement from additive detail.** Mixed-grain assignments replace overlapping assignments. Direct detail records contribute through configured aggregation. Expose resolution counts, not “0 mapped keys” for engine-generated keys; retain compatible legacy selector reads without offering new Level/Value authoring. **Reason:** these are two legitimate, different fact contracts.

- **X10 — Transaction-first onboarding.** New Transactions default to Create Dimension in a two-option mapping switch; the alternative is Use Existing Dimension, with a Resolve multi-grain data checkbox switching between direct mapping and multi-grain configuration. Create Dimension supports Pivot and Date resolution before choosing grain columns from the effective output and a level name, including when other levels already exist. Snapshot those input stages in the source population under a stable output-ID namespace so independent rebuilds remain reproducible without a Transaction dependency cycle. Preview is read-only; Save creates an ordinary distinct-source Dimension and directly mapped Transaction, Build Semantic later prepares its population. Do not create extra member catalogs, a new persistent mapping mode or an automatic union. Select Base only for the first level when unset; preserve existing Base. Exact retries reuse definitions; build failure leaves explicit saved/not-built state. Allow explicit later source-field remapping while preserving grain IDs. **Reason:** one-table users can begin without manually authoring structural objects, while population ownership, identity and existing engine semantics stay explicit.


- **X11 — Optional measures during creation.** Select numeric transformed output columns in a compact grid with Editable, explicit Non-time/Time aggregation and Dis-Aggregation (default Weighted by self). Restrict multi-grain selections to configured resolved values. Derive names, Transaction/value/native references and fixed null/order defaults. Save all definitions atomically, including any generated level; previews create none and Materialise stays separate. **Reason:** common referenced measures need minimal repeated input without introducing partial metadata or changing transaction allocation semantics.

### Date ranges and periods

- **R01 — Ordered, independent calendar stage.** Transaction stages are Pivot → Date resolution → Dimension mapping/resolution → Filters. Each stage consumes the preceding effective schema and leaves raw/shared managed data unchanged. **Reason:** calendar fields and explicit attribute columns must exist before matching can use them.
- **R02 — Inclusive daily expansion.** Map start/end to the daily Time Date column; bring selected calendar fields into the Transaction under stable IDs. Preserve source values unchanged at this stage. **Reason:** resolving period membership is separate from allocating a quantity or percentage.
- **R03 — Keep complete Time identity.** Map all required period keys, such as Week and Month. A Jan 30–Feb 2 range can contribute to both Week/January and Week/February intersections. **Reason:** deduplicating Week alone can erase valid period segments.
- **R04 — Resolve days before period folding.** Apply mixed-grain precedence per native key and day, so a later partial-period assignment affects only its own days. The measure's Time policy then folds daily contributions to the native period, respecting axis order. **Reason:** selecting one weekly winner or hard-coding SUM would misstate partial promotions and rates.
- **R05 — Reject invalid resolution.** Missing/invalid/reversed/out-of-calendar endpoints, no eligible dates, incompatible mappings and excessive expansion fail clearly. Sparse calendars define eligible days; no synthetic dates. Ordinary date-range resolution has no automatic DateTime/timezone contract. **Reason:** plausible-looking period output must not hide missing coverage or ambiguous temporal meaning.

### Final-output filters

- **F01 — Optional final predicates.** Dimension filters run after Pivot/aliasing; Planning filters after population/enrichment; Transaction filters after mapping, splitting and winners, before measure aggregation. **Reason:** users filter the resulting data, and removing a winner must not resurrect an older assignment or change original split denominators.
- **F02 — Typed scalar operators.** Support string equality/list/contains/prefix/suffix and negative forms; blank/not blank; numeric/date comparisons and inclusive ranges/list tests; boolean true/false; NULL tests for all. Text matching is case-sensitive and wildcard characters are literal. **Reason:** predictable typed predicates avoid raw SQL and accidental coercion.
- **F03 — Explicit NULL and grouping.** Support nested AND/OR combinations with visible parentheses/summary. Ordinary predicates, including negative ones, exclude NULL; include it through an explicit NULL branch. List values are one literal per line, preserving commas. **Reason:** OR scope, missing values and list parsing materially change selected business rows.
- **F04 — Schema-bound, bounded authoring.** Offer preceding-output columns by stable ID, including unexposed Dimension fields and retained/generated Transaction fields. Missing columns, incompatible operators and malformed values fail; Clear all removes filtering. Bound nesting to 16 levels, 100 children/group, 1,000 nodes and 100 values/list. **Reason:** filters remain understandable, safe to compile and explicit about unavailable data.

### Measure loading and policy

- **M01 — Reference or calculation, one native grain.** A referenced measure inherits Output Dimension from its Transaction; a calculated measure selects it. Present Reference/Calculated choices explicitly and highlight Output Dimension. Reference authoring selects Transaction and Value Column; Editable precedes Missing transaction value. Changing a displayed grouping does not redefine authority. **Reason:** storage location and business-native grain are different concepts.
- **M02 — Policies are saved, not guessed.** Use configured NonTime and Time aggregation and order. The current authoring preset is SUM/SUM, Ignore NULLs, All-null NULL; it is a starting choice, not a universal quantity/percentage rule. **Reason:** names and presumed business common sense cannot determine a safe aggregation.
- **M03 — Supported aggregation and ordering.** NonTime supports SUM/AVG/MIN/MAX/COUNT/COUNT DISTINCT; Time also supports FIRST/LAST using declared ordering. Default stage order is NonTime then Time, with Time then NonTime also supported. **Reason:** mixed axes and irregular intersections can make these operations noncommutative.
- **M04 — Preserve source NULLs.** Source NULL policy is always Preserve and hidden from the UI; old Reject metadata is interpreted compatibly. **Reason:** source missingness must reach the configured aggregation/nullability policies instead of introducing a competing selector.
- **M05 — Aggregation NULL handling.** Ignore aggregates non-NULL inputs; Propagate returns NULL when any input is NULL. With Ignore and no populated input, All-null result chooses NULL or zero; Propagate takes precedence. **Reason:** partial missingness and an entirely missing group have different business interpretations.
- **M06 — Missing facts differ from NULL facts.** Missing transaction value chooses NULL/zero for an absent native group after the LEFT JOIN, separately from a present group whose value is NULL. Nonnullable result declarations reject remaining NULLs. **Reason:** “no transaction” must not be silently treated as “known zero.”
- **M07 — Source builds derive finer visibility from aggregation.** On each expanding SUM axis split equally over eligible finer combinations; for other supported methods repeat. Use the matching Time/NonTime policy and reverse aggregation order. Counts follow actual distinct intermediate intersections, not theoretical members. **Reason:** source initialisation must work without prior edit weights and must respect the declared aggregation stages.
- **M08 — Initialisation is not edit allocation.** Source builds ignore allocation mode, prior self values and basis measures. Base-native measures load normally; AggregateFirst resolves dependencies instead of splitting its formula result. At the end of the measure form, a read-only Base Level Visibility explanation describes the proven path and applicable split/repeat behaviour when a different Base is selected, or explains why the path is unavailable. **Reason:** a legitimate first build must not fail because no previous built self-weight pattern exists.
- **M09 — Do not invent inverse/conservation rules.** Stage-wise equal shares need not be globally equal on irregular intersections. Repeat followed by SUM can increase a total; COUNT/distinct and AVG-of-averages are not generally invertible/composable. Do not substitute AVG/SUM or rebalance a final child. **Reason:** saved semantics must take precedence over an assumed conservation law.
- **M10 — Preserve independent natives.** A Base-connected measure is loaded as a private native seed, brought to Base where possible and derived to compatible defined levels, including native. If Base is unreachable, keep native and supported direct coarser paths. Native execution failure is fatal; unsupported other representations have reasons. **Reason:** connectivity must not fabricate values or hide a valid independent planning domain.
- **M11 — Numeric representation is explicit.** Referenced values use Float64, widening before aggregation/distribution; new calculations default to Float64. Legacy integer declarations normalise, explicit Decimal calculations retain their contract, and nonnumeric/non-finite/invalid results fail. Hide type/precision/scale controls. **Reason:** integer-source allocation must allow fractions without pretending Float64 is exact decimal arithmetic.
- **M12 — Storage and display precision differ.** Round measure representations to six places at representation boundaries with bankers rounding; display Float/Decimal measure values to two places. Edit the original stored value, not the rounded text. Source rows/identifier formatting stay intact; no hidden rounding-remainder redistribution. Unit/Alias controls stay hidden without discarding compatible stored metadata. **Reason:** readable displays must not corrupt later edits or invent exactness/units.

- **M10 — Typed values and attribute calculations.** Support inferred Number/String/Date/Boolean references and formula results. Transaction fields require referenced measures; Dimension/Dimension attributes can be referenced directly when functionally determined by output grain. Restrict policies by type, preserve Mixed separately from NULL, and keep nonnumeric outputs read-only with no finer-level distribution. AggregateFirst may recalculate only from available inputs. Preserve numeric behavior and the existing population/value table pair. **Reason:** statuses, dates and exception flags are valid planning outputs but quantities' summation and allocation semantics do not apply.

### Calculated measures

- **C01 — Stored semantic expressions.** Store a versioned typed expression with stable dependency IDs and descriptive text, not executable SQL or a serialised SQLGlot tree. Validate functions, arity, types, dependency availability and cycles. **Reason:** labels can change and agents/compilers need one portable meaning.
- **C02 — AggregateFirst.** Resolve each operand at the requested grain using its own policies, then calculate. Reuse a stored calculated value only at equivalent exact grain; otherwise recalculate recursively. **Reason:** summing absolute errors can differ from absolute error of aggregated Forecast/Sales.
- **C03 — CalculateFirst.** Evaluate natively, then propagate/aggregate its result using the calculated measure's policies. Valid calculated-on-calculated chains are supported. **Reason:** row-level derived quantities need a different semantic contract from an aggregate-level ratio/error.
- **C04 — Explicit missingness and controls.** Formula NULLs propagate unless an explicit operation such as COALESCE changes that meaning. Calculated measures are read-only. AggregateFirst has no allocation controls; Base-output calculated measures hide disaggregation controls, while referenced Base measures retain them for coarser edits. **Reason:** formula outputs are derived, and control visibility must not erase a referenced measure's future edit policy.

### Build and materialisation

- **B01 — Save definitions, preview queries.** Source-backed Dimension Save commits metadata only. Multiple-table and existing-Dimension Save also builds the derived population, preparing required ancestors first and reusing current tables; it does not build measures. An unchanged save preserves definition versions. Preview and schema analysis support unbuilt parent chains without saving or creating tables. **Reason:** attribute selection and inspection must work before physical tables exist, while derived Dimensions should have a usable population after Save.
- **B02 — Durable, ordered population preparation.** Build Semantic resolves dependencies, reuses ready populations and builds missing/stale Dimensions before measure values. The coordinator and children resume through fenced jobs; definition changes supersede the build. **Reason:** one action must create a usable network without depending on an open browser.
- **B03 — Incremental build, explicit refresh and separate inspection.** Decision View exposes incremental Build Semantic Network and confirmed Full Semantic Refresh above the pivot. The Materialised table selector below the pivot lists built tables and loads a preview on selection. There is no separate Build Semantic navigation item. Saved valid definitions are required; the engine prepares populations automatically and applies existing optional Base rules. **Reason:** preview selection must not imply a restricted build scope.
- **B04 — Coherent activation and explicit rebuild effects.** Activate all affected network levels together only after checks succeed; failure/cancellation/staleness preserves the prior active generation. A no-change incremental build reuses the active generation. Added/changed measures invalidate only their affected representations and dependent formulas; unchanged edits are preserved. Full Refresh recomputes sources and replaces manual edits; registered source/population changes recompute affected measures. Publication requires current managed/population artifacts, not measure materialisation. **Reason:** partial generations or implied edit replay would mislead planners.

### Decision View

- **V01 — Server-calculated pivot.** Select exposed Dimension attributes or explicit Dimension grain/population attributes for Rows/Columns and ordered measures for Values; measures display as rows within dimension groups. Reorder/remove selections. Empty axes request a global group; the browser arranges results without calculating aggregates/formulas. **Reason:** policy semantics must remain identical across UI and other consumers.
- **V02 — Correct source selection before optimisation.** Require coherent current population/network inputs and all fields/measures or dependencies. Prefer an exact eligible grain, then deterministic smaller candidates. Coarser Base reuse requires complete unfiltered projection ancestry; independent/native fallback may supply otherwise unavailable measures. **Reason:** a smaller table is useful only if it represents the requested population correctly.
- **V03 — Safe further aggregation only.** Exact stored values can be reused; further coarse-cache composition needs a proof. Current conservative reuse allows compatible SUM/MIN/MAX cases, with NULL-policy exceptions; AVG, counts, mixed policies and unsafe filtered subsets fall back to a suitable finer/Base source or fail. **Reason:** aggregating stored aggregates is not generally equivalent to aggregating underlying values.
- **V04 — Experience memory is local.** Persist ordered row/column/measure selections per model/revision in browser memory/storage, not results or semantic definitions. Restore fresh results on re-entry. Only Clear selections or clearing browser storage clears the layout. Rebuilds preserve valid selections and refresh values; removed references are discarded. **Reason:** navigation should not force repeated setup or reuse stale values.
- **V05 — Explanations and bounded output.** Show selected source level, reuse/recalculation, unavailability and edit reasons; distinguish NULL, empty members and absent intersections. Reject oversized queries instead of truncating business totals. Query-time filters, automatic grand totals/subtotals and shared saved views are not delivered. **Reason:** users need trustworthy results and honest capability boundaries.

### Interactive edits

- **W01 — Authority determines eligibility.** Only editable referenced measures can be changed, at their native or proven coarser displayed grain, including supported ad hoc/global groupings. Finer views, calculations, absent intersections, published revisions and stale data are read-only. **Reason:** a finer physical representation does not authorise inventing finer business decisions.
- **W02 — Choose a reachable write grain.** Write to Base when a proven finer/equivalent path connects it appropriately to native and display; otherwise write to native and supported coarser levels. Do not require a Base path for independent measures. **Reason:** the PLC versus PLT case must remain usable without creating a fictitious Time/Customer relationship.
- **W03 — An edit is an absolute value.** Resolve the full eligible write population, independent of visible pagination. None/null repeats; Equal divides; Weighted uses captured basis values; Weighted by self uses captured populated self values. Freeze weights before any write. New authoring defaults to self-weighted; legacy omitted policy retains Repeat. **Reason:** the entered value is not universally an additive delta/total, and partially updated weights create order-dependent allocations.
- **W04 — Sparse weights are intentional.** Self-weighting allocates only across non-NULL populated cells and leaves missing cells unchanged; zero-valued cells keep zero if the group total is nonzero. Missing or zero-total patterns fail without silent Equal/Repeat fallback. **Reason:** a sparse promotion horizon is legitimate, but an absent distribution cannot justify a guessed one.
- **W05 — Recompute complete groups and dependencies.** Preserve unaffected cells/measures; aggregate complete groups, including unchanged members, to compatible coarser levels. Recalculate AggregateFirst at each target and CalculateFirst natively before propagation. A basis change influences subsequent allocations, not automatic reallocation of unrelated referenced values. **Reason:** partial updates and unintended secondary allocation would corrupt existing plans.
- **W06 — Versioned, atomic edit completion.** Validate displayed revision/generation and eligibility on the server. Activate affected levels coherently; stale/conflicting/failed edits leave prior bindings. Save/Enter submits, Cancel/Escape discards unsent input; distinguish a committed edit whose UI refresh failed from a failed write. **Reason:** a stale pivot or ambiguous success must not overwrite a newer plan or provoke duplicate edits.
- **W07 — No durable business edit journal.** Active edited values survive restart and incremental reuse. Full Refresh replaces edits, while source/semantic/population changes replace affected representations. Transient job recovery input is not a replayable audit/override store. **Reason:** this scope explicitly excludes durable edit replay, and neither provenance nor persistence promises may imply it exists.

### Engineering invariants that affect product correctness

- **E01 — One shared engine, modular contexts.** UI captures intent; context API/service classes coordinate it; repositories own metadata persistence; reusable planners/compilers/executors own SQL and execution. Pivot, Filters, populations, calendar, resolution, measures and edits compose through shared contracts. **Reason:** feature-specific SQL services would diverge on types, grain and policy semantics.
- **E02 — Separate meaning from data and operations.** MongoDB holds revision-owned semantic definitions, evidence/examples/vocabulary and operational manifests. ClickHouse holds raw data, managed views, populations and values. Connections stay server-owned; meanings are shared across scenarios rather than scenario-specific definitions. Evidence is not silently treated as verified merely because it was supplied. **Reason:** agents and humans need durable intent with honest provenance, not duplicated connection or scenario state.
- **E03 — One permanent table per materialised dimension.** Keys, attributes and measure columns share one physical table and a stable ordinary view. Source-backed lookup dimensions use source views without copied populations. Temporary build clones/candidates are permitted and removed after activation. **Reason:** avoid duplicate population/value storage while retaining safe rebuilds.
- **E04 — Clone, validate, exchange.** Build affected measures in isolation, record publication UUIDs, exchange validated tables and commit coherent bindings behind the read/edit barrier. Remove old copies after success and recover interrupted swaps by UUID. No physical row-generation column is needed. Choose sorting from initial grain-key cardinalities, with explicit time keys first, and persist it. **Reason:** preserve live data on build failure and accelerate suitable reads without retaining historical datasets. Exchange is metadata work, not a promise that rebuilding is constant-time.
- **E05 — Durable jobs and guarded concurrency.** Use scoped idempotency, expected object/revision versions, transactionally captured inputs, renewable worker leases/fences and read retention. Worker recovery must not activate stale results. **Reason:** duplicate requests, reloads, crashes and concurrent changes cannot safely rely on in-process state alone.
- **E06 — Typed plans and honest snapshots.** Compile allowlisted typed metadata/semantic AST to safely quoted ClickHouse SQL; no user SQL or executable vocabulary text. Captured manifests freeze definitions/artifact identities, not arbitrary external row changes across systems. **Reason:** syntax safety is not semantic validation, and metadata hashes are not warehouse-wide transactional snapshots.
- **E07 — Bounds and failure isolation.** Validate full relevant inputs/candidates, retain bounded diagnostics and enforce row/memory/time/SQL/expansion limits. A failed build/read must not be presented as success over a truncated result. **Reason:** predictable resource use cannot come at the cost of silent partial plans.

### Operations and destructive actions

- **O01 — Supported local startup, explicit production boundary.** Compose starts health-checked UI/API/MongoDB/ClickHouse with persistent volumes. Production mode requires its API credential; external deployment still needs TLS, user access, HA/backup/retention and operational decisions. **Reason:** an easy evaluation install is not a claim of enterprise deployment readiness.
- **O02 — Whole-workspace reset.** Nuke everything uses one explicit confirmation, applies across models/revisions and deletes all application data and tables/views in the configured ClickHouse database. Recreate only the default model/draft, with no published pointer, Base or objects. Keep configuration/indexes and protect unrelated databases. **Reason:** this is a predictable fresh-start tool, not a model-scoped deletion or an accidental migration path.
- **O03 — Maintenance is coordinated.** Reset closes admission, drains/fences work and stays in maintenance after partial physical failure until recovery succeeds. One-time existing table-name migration is operator-run, not startup/service behaviour. **Reason:** background work must not repopulate or reference a half-reset/migrated workspace.

## 4. The three distribution contracts

These examples are illustrative, not a new default policy:

1. **Transaction resolution:** Brand A/Zone North has quantity 100 and matches P1/L1 and P2/L1. Split gives 50/50; a later P1/L1 assignment of 30 produces 30/50, not 30/70. A still later coarse assignment may win again. Copy would repeat 100 before overlay. This follows X06–X08.
2. **Initial materialisation:** a coarse SUM value 100 with four eligible finer combinations in one expansion stage produces 25 each, without consulting self weights. AVG or another supported non-SUM method repeats. Multiple axes follow their declared stages; do not promise equal final shares across irregular multi-axis intersections. This follows M07–M09.
3. **Interactive edit:** populated self weights 20, 30, NULL and an edited value of 100 produce 40, 60, NULL under self-weighting. A constant percentage edited to 10 under None/Repeat becomes 10 in every eligible finer cell; its subsequent aggregate follows the configured methods, not an inferred AVG. This follows W03–W04.

For copied daily value 10 over three days in W1 and four in W2, native Time SUM gives 30/40; native Time AVG gives 10/10. This fold is separate from both source-to-Base initialisation and later edit allocation.

## 5. Product limits and scalability requirements

These are current configurable/schema bounds, not latency/throughput SLAs. Preserve explicit failure and operator visibility when a supported workload exceeds them.

- **Authoring:** default 1 MiB metadata request and 5,000 objects/revision; bounded cursor pagination. **Reason:** metadata retrieval and validation must not require one unbounded document/request.
- **Pivot:** up to 20 pairs, 200 outputs/pair, 32 retained/grouping/key columns and 2,000 outputs. **Reason:** wide schemas and discovery must stay bounded; selecting the first page silently is invalid.
- **Resolution:** up to 32 axes, eight rules/axis, 16 total rules and 32 selected numeric values; current defaults 64 observed profiles and one million expanded matches. The UI uses one order column and no independent partition/tie-breaker authoring. **Reason:** selective observed patterns are tractable; enumerating every possible attribute combination is not.
- **Planning populations:** default one million rows. Library planning byte cap is 512 MiB; current root Compose overrides it to 2 GiB. The cap can cover retained/candidate physical-table data, so rebuilds need headroom. **Reason:** the strict table pair does not bound retained rows or intermediate Cartesian/query memory.
- **Measure compilation:** default 1,024 plan nodes, 4 MiB generated SQL and four ClickHouse query threads, alongside warehouse execution bounds. **Reason:** formulas and resolution can grow even when final tables are small.
- **Decision View:** eight attributes across axes, twelve measures, default 5,000 groups and 50,000 cells. The 50-row preview sample limit is not a limit of 50 planning groups. **Reason:** a complete bounded pivot is preferable to misleading truncated totals.
- **Retention/recovery:** leases, pins, generation collection and the documented grace period protect active reads/builds. Long-term operational history, abandoned CSV staging cleanup and large-catalog strategies still need capacity policy. **Reason:** table-count bounds alone do not bound storage growth.

See [Operations](OPERATIONS.md) and [Engine/storage](ENGINE_AND_STORAGE.md) for authoritative deployment settings and recovery details. Do not raise a product limit by removing correctness checks.

## 6. Acceptance criteria

### Release completion gate

The current-scope walkthrough is complete when its definitions can be reopened, populations and network can be built coherently, supported views produce policy-correct results, and eligible edits update dependent measures. The acceptance cases below must also exercise the failure branches; a successful happy-path video alone is insufficient. Product review must keep the unresolved work in section 8 explicit rather than interpreting the word production as an availability or deployment guarantee.

### Acceptance scenarios

Use a disposable fixture with active/inactive Product members, long Location attributes, a daily fiscal calendar, valid Product/Location pairs, uniform Forecast and coarse Sales, overlapping mixed-grain Promotions and at least one independent native level. Exact row counts should be derived from that fixture, not copied from the demo.

1. **A01 — Context and retry:** fresh bootstrap yields one model/draft and no publication; a second bootstrap is idempotent. Import a batch with one malformed file; successful files remain, only unsuccessful entries retry, and collisions never overwrite. Covers P01–P04, S01–S05.
2. **A02 — Active Dimension:** filter active members, unexpose the validity flag and reopen. The filter still applies; raw source remains unchanged; keys remain exposed. A population referencing a removed member fails membership rather than silently losing rows. Covers D01–D04, F01.
3. **A03 — Both long formats:** merge separate Location records by location_id, but keep same-record Forecast Product/Location pairs together with empty separate-record keys. Identical quantities at different Brand/Zone coordinates survive. Duplicate selected EAV cells and conflicting retained fields fail. Covers T01–T05.
4. **A04 — Population identity:** build valid Product/Location × Time and derive Brand/Zone/period by parent projection. Daily repetitions collapse. A nonunique period label or varying non-key Month fails until identity/selection is corrected. Optional membership/enrichment do not change grain. Covers L01–L06.
5. **A05 — Authority and paths:** uniform coarse Sales stays coarse-native and receives Base visibility only with a proof. An independent PLT measure with PLC Base remains native/coarser and editable where eligible. Incompatible combinations give reasons. Covers L07–L09, M10, W01–W02.
6. **A06 — No fact multiplication:** multiple detail rows in two Transactions aggregate independently before joining. Missing fact groups and present NULL groups obey their separate policies. Integer-source 1 split across two eligible rows can become 0.5 each. Covers X02–X03, M04–M06, M11.
7. **A07 — Precedence and denominators:** verify coarse then detail and the reverse order; include ties, missing constraints and a NULL winner. With 100 split across two cells followed by detail 30, result is 30/50. A filter removing the winner does not restore the old row. Covers X04–X09, F01.
8. **A08 — Calendar periods:** cross a month boundary, use composite Week/Month keys and apply a later partial-period promotion. Check Copy and Split independently across members/dates, then SUM/AVG/FIRST/LAST period folds using calendar order. Reject bad ranges and uncovered mappings. Covers R01–R05.
9. **A09 — Filters:** author nested AND/OR using String, Numeric, Bool and Date/DateTime fields; save/reopen and compare preview/build output. Verify NULL versus blank, negative predicates, literal commas/wildcards, stale columns and bounds. Intermediate previews exclude final filters; normal preview includes them with a correct total. Covers F01–F04, S06.
10. **A10 — Initialisation versus edits:** a first self-weight-configured coarse measure builds without previous weights. SUM stage initialisation splits and other methods repeat. A later edit uses its explicit allocation; sparse self weights preserve NULLs and missing/zero patterns fail. Covers M07–M09, W03–W04.
11. **A11 — Formula timing:** with detail Forecast 100/80 and Sales 90/100, AggregateFirst absolute error at the combined grain is 10; CalculateFirst with SUM yields 30. Validate the calculated-on-calculated promotion chain and reject cycles/unavailable inputs. Covers C01–C04.
12. **A12 — Rates and dependent edits:** explicitly use repeated editable percentage and suitable AVG policies. Forecast 1,150 at 10% yields reduction 115 and final 1,035. A Sales edit from 721 to 750 against Forecast 758 changes Error 37 to 8. Finer Sales views remain read-only. Covers M02–M03, V01, W01–W06.
13. **A13 — Correct read source:** exact coarse caches may be reused; unsafe average/count/filtered-cache compositions fall back or fail, without changing the measure's policy. Reordering measure/axis selections changes display order, not values. Covers V01–V03.
14. **A14 — State and failures:** preview/build samples are bounded but validation is complete. Dimension Preview queries without committing or building; Save commits metadata. Stale metadata, worker recovery, cancellation or failed multi-level activation preserve prior bindings and unaffected cells. Covers P07–P08, B01–B04, E03–E07, W05–W06.
15. **A15 — Edit lifecycle:** an edit survives restart and refresh; a later source network rebuild replaces it. Successful builds preserve valid browser layout selections and reload fresh values, while failed builds and edits do not clear selections. No replay/journal or server-shared view is implied. Covers V04, W07.
16. **A16 — Storage and governance:** adding/removing/rebuilding measures never creates extra planning/value tables. The default remains editable, removed lifecycle endpoints are absent from production OpenAPI, and retained historical definitions stay immutable. A reset requires one confirmation and recreates only a fresh default draft after coordinated cleanup. Covers P03–P06, E02–E05, O01–O03.

17. **A17 — Table-first analysis:** start with one Forecast table and zero Dimensions/levels; preview without saving, create/build its distinct grain, verify automatic mappings/Base and plot/edit a referenced measure through dimension axes. Repeated fact rows aggregate without fanout. Link Sales to the same level; extra intersections fail until the user explicitly replaces/expands the population. Replacement preserves grain/mapping IDs, and exact creation retries create no duplicates. Covers X10, L01, X02–X03, V01 and W01–W06.

## 7. Success measures and release evidence

Track successful completion of the walkthrough without custom entity SQL; definition-to-first-valid-network time; correction rate by validation category; explained versus silent failures; eligible edit success/conflict rate; safe materialised-source reuse versus correctness fallback; and desktop/mobile task completion. These are proposed product measures, not a claim that analytics instrumentation already exists.

Hard correctness gates are no silent truncation, no arbitrary duplicate winner, no finer-than-native edit, no partial active network and no cross-revision/reference corruption. Performance/availability targets require an agreed workload and production deployment profile; do not invent response-time or concurrency guarantees from this local demo.

Release evidence should combine typed/schema/API tests, real ClickHouse checks, transaction/lease/activation recovery tests and browser save/reopen/mobile tests for the acceptance cases. Data-changing tests must use disposable fixtures, not a user's workspace.

## 8. Open decisions and deliberate follow-ups

- **Development/production promotion:** revision cloning/publication exists, but environment ownership, migration, release approvals and source binding across environments need their own contract.
- **Source refresh and master-data editing:** decide how supported updates preserve IDs and invalidate generations, and how row edits interact with filters/intersections. No current raw-row editing or automatic reconciliation promise.
- **Drill To:** review PK/FK ownership, business roles, explicit navigation paths, carry/replace filter behaviour and related-record versus exact-contribution semantics before implementation. Planning-grain compatibility does not solve these automatically.
- **Edit durability and provenance:** current no-journal scope is explicit. Prominent product guidance should state that Full Refresh replaces manual edits and incremental invalidation can replace affected measures; confirmation and field help explain the difference. Replayable overrides, exact contribution drill for edited cells and business edit audit are separate requirements.
- **Rates, exact decimals and population scope:** aggregation is explicitly chosen. Define stronger rate/weight/precision contracts only when required; a matching grain alone does not prove Forecast and Sales cover comparable business populations.
- **Consumer views:** query-time filters, automatic totals/subtotals, shared layouts, plan scenarios and multi-user identity remain separate work, not extensions silently enabled by metadata flags.
- **Production operations:** decide HA, backup/restore, TLS/user identity, retention, capacity, measured performance targets and monitoring. The current engine's modularity and safeguards are necessary foundations, not substitutes for these decisions.

### Versioned formula authoring and counts

- **C07 — Backend language authority.** Lark parses `planning-v1`; stable semantic AST v2 retains resolved IDs, while SQLGlot generates dialect SQL. Validate on typing and re-resolve on save. Legacy ASTs remain compatible. **Reason:** one language contract across clients and future execution backends.
- **C08 — Count at Output Dimension.** COUNT, COUNT_IF, COUNT_DISTINCT and COUNT_ROWS evaluate at the measure’s Output Dimension, then recompute for each supported output/view grain. They are read-only AggregateFirst formulas with no result allocation. **Reason:** counting aggregated values or summing distinct counts changes meaning.
- **C09 — Population versus transaction counts.** COUNT_ROWS counts valid planning intersections; COUNT skips NULL but includes zero/False. Empty groups return zero; unknown/Mixed arguments remain unknown/Mixed. **Reason:** native aggregation has already combined raw transaction records and must not pretend to recover them.

- **C10 — Inline formula input library.** Replace function/attribute dropdowns with searchable Measures, Columns and Functions tabs beside the editor. Group columns by semantic owner, show function syntax/examples, preserve caret/selection, support keyboard navigation and show backend validation/type feedback. Reuse a compact version in existing Quick Create. **Reason:** users can compose and learn expressions without losing their editing position or opening another modal.

### Transaction-driven materialisation and Decision View attributes

Native Transaction Dimensions and optional Base are the shared measure storage targets. Own native/output storage remains mandatory; other native targets accept only proven rollups from native/Base using configured policies. Unused lookup Dimensions do not cause extra measure representations. Calculated-output Dimensions remain stored for their own calculations. No user-facing materialisation property is added. **Reason:** keep the simplified Dimension workflow without multiplying storage for every lookup.

Decision View offers the union of exposed native/Base attributes, including Brand from PLW or BZM. Proven aliases are deduplicated and stored browser selections remain restorable; equal labels alone are insufficient. Selected measures must support the requested attributes through explicit metadata paths. **Reason:** offer usable reporting fields without inventing relationships or changing aggregation mathematics.
